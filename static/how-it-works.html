What it does

HitPay Content Creator is an internal content tool for the marketing team. It uses Claude AI (claude-sonnet-4-6) to produce SEO- and AEO-optimised, market-accurate blog posts for Southeast Asia, repurpose them into social content, and manage the full editorial workflow from draft to publication.

Posts are written for three markets: 🇸🇬 SG 🇲🇾 MY 🇵🇭 PH — or all three at once if no market is selected. The brand switcher in the header targets either HitPay or SME Growth Hub, each with its own knowledge docs, internal link index, blog domain, and social scheduling. Finished posts export as CSV for Framer CMS, and edited CSVs can be re-imported to sync fields back into a post.

Three creation modes
✦
Generate New Post — enter a keyword or topic. The AI researches, structures, and writes a full post from scratch with all optimisations applied. Tagged New in the post list.
↺
Rewrite Existing Post — paste any blog post URL. The AI scrapes the existing content, then rewrites it with improved structure, SEO, AEO optimisation, and updated HitPay positioning. The source URL is stored and shown in the editor sidebar. Tagged Rewrite.
🚀
From Product Launch — paste a raw PRD or launch EDM. The AI treats it as the primary source of truth, strips internal-only notes ("Note for the team", "Limitations at Launch"), and generates a full blog post plus matching X, Threads, and LinkedIn drafts in one pass. Tagged 🚀 Launch. Each piece (blog, social, LinkedIn) can also be generated as a separate step, since long-running single requests can drop on Vercel's serverless runtime.
Data sources used during generation
1
HitPay MCP server — queries the internal knowledge base across general product guide categories (up to 11 results). Grounds the AI in accurate HitPay features, flows, and merchant use cases.
2
Product documentation — hitpay_docs.md (or sme_growth_hub_docs.md for the SME Growth Hub brand) is split into sections and scored by keyword relevance. The top sections (up to 30,000 chars) are injected for accurate feature names, flows, and pricing.
3
Competitor research database — competitors/*.json profiles payment competitors by features, markets, and weaknesses. The top 5 relevant profiles are matched by market signal and keyword overlap for positioning context.
4
Internal blog link index — blog_links.yaml (brand-specific, filtered by market) lists all published posts by topic. The AI picks exactly 3 genuinely relevant URLs and weaves them as natural in-content hyperlinks — never as a list.
What the AI is instructed to do

Every post is generated with a detailed system prompt. Key rules:

  • Lead with the reader's problem — not HitPay's features. Product gets mentioned in 1–2 sections at most.
  • 900–1,200 words of body content (excluding the FAQ), 3–5 H2 sections, sparingly-used H3s. No H1 (CMS adds it).
  • No marketing language — "seamlessly", "unlock", "revolutionise", "game-changer/game-changing", "cutting-edge", "empower", "leverage" are explicitly banned in favour of specific, verifiable claims.
  • Market-accurate payment methods — per-market lists of valid methods, plus a fact-check list of cross-market misattributions to avoid (e.g. PayNow is SG-only).
  • Local specificity — district and city names required per market (Tanjong Pagar, Bangsar, BGC).
  • No fabricated stats or testimonials — all claims must come from the knowledge base.
  • Exactly 3 internal backlinks woven naturally using descriptive anchor text — 3 well-placed relevant links outperform 5 forced ones for GEO citability.
  • Payout messaging — domestic transactions settle "next business day" in SG, MY & PH; cross-border payments settle T+2. Always distinguish the two when relevant.
  • Competitor comparisons — factual, non-disparaging. Every competitor gets a "Best for:" line that is true but narrow enough that most SMB readers self-disqualify from it, while HitPay's own "Best for" stays broad and inclusive. No fake data.
AEO — AI Answer Engine Optimisation

Every post is structured to rank in AI-generated answers (Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, ChatGPT, Claude). Rules applied automatically:

  • Quick Answer block (required, always first) — a bold-prefixed 2–3 sentence block before any intro prose that names HitPay and the relevant market(s), self-contained enough for an AI engine to quote on its own.
  • H2/H3 headings as questions — "What is…", "How does…", "Which… is best for…" — the format AI engines use to pull FAQ-style answers.
  • Mandatory FAQ section — at least 5 Q&A pairs at the end, each a self-contained 2–4 sentence answer that opens with a declarative statement rather than "Yes, you can…".
  • Numbered and bulleted lists — for any step-by-step processes or comparisons, improving extractability.
  • Comparison tables — when comparing HitPay to alternatives, uses Markdown tables AI engines can parse.
  • Cited data points — real numbers (fees, limits, timelines) stated plainly so they can be quoted.
  • [SCHEMA] block — each post includes a JSON-LD schema block (FAQPage always; HowTo or Product added when relevant) for structured data markup.
Generation pipeline (in order)
1
Query HitPay MCP knowledge base (general + product + guide)
2
Score and inject relevant doc sections from hitpay_docs.md
3
Match competitor profiles by keyword + market from competitors/*.json
4
Build internal backlink reference from blog_links.yaml and select 3 external links using the GEO authority strategy (see below)
5
Rewrite only: scrape the source URL, extract title and body text. Product Launch only: pass the pasted PRD/EDM text in as primary source material instead.
6
Send all context to claude-sonnet-4-6 with the full system prompt (SEO + AEO + competitor rules), streaming the response with exponential backoff retry if the API is overloaded
7
Parse JSON response → save to Supabase + open in editor. Product Launch only: also kicks off matching X, Threads, and LinkedIn drafts in parallel.
GEO link strategy — why articles are linked the way they are

GEO (Generative Engine Optimisation) is about getting HitPay content cited by AI answer engines — Perplexity, ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Claude. AI engines evaluate outbound links as a trust signal: linking to authoritative, diverse sources makes content more citable. Linking to the same partner blog every article signals shallow sourcing and reduces citability.

Internal backlinks: 3 (not 5)

Three highly relevant internal links are better than five padded ones. AI engines evaluate anchor text relevance — a forced link with weak context actively lowers the article's authority signal. The AI is instructed to only link where it is genuinely relevant, never to hit a number.

External links: always 3, fixed slot roles

1
Regulator (always) — MAS for SG, Bank Negara Malaysia for MY, BSP for PH. Government and central bank URLs are the strongest authority signal available. This slot is never skipped.
2
Official source — the most keyword-relevant payment method page (e.g. PayNow, DuitNow, GCash official pages) or, for integration articles, the relevant platform's documentation. First-party official pages, not blog posts.
3
Research / data source — rotates deterministically by keyword across: Singapore Department of Statistics, PayNet Malaysia, Philippine Statistics Authority, Statista market reports, World Bank financial inclusion data, Google-Temasek e-Conomy SEA. This ensures no two articles cite the same research source, which AI engines read as breadth of evidence.

Comparison articles — slots 2 and 3 are filled with competitor blog articles (one per competitor, from the scraped article database), so competitor links appear with specific context rather than just a homepage. All competitor links use rel="nofollow" and must only appear in sentences directly about that specific competitor.

Competitor links appear no earlier than 30% into the article. Placing a competitor link in the intro or first section causes readers to click away before they've engaged with the HitPay article. The AI is instructed to place all competitor links in the second half of the body — inside comparison or context sections — never in the opening.

Why not Shopify and WooCommerce blog posts? Partner blog articles are weak GEO signals — AI engines interpret repeated links to the same partner blog as affiliate bias, not genuine research. Shopify and WooCommerce only appear as external links when the article is specifically about Shopify or WooCommerce integration (slot 2, official docs URL only — not a blog post).

Repurposing into social content

Any published post (or a raw EDM newsletter) can be repurposed into platform-native social drafts, each with its own dedicated editor and scheduling queue:

𝕏
X (Twitter) — generates three angle "choices" (quick win, thread, contextual take) with multiple hook variants each, written in HitPay's real @officialhitpay voice — terse, technical, product-team narration (a reframe punchline, a concrete micro-scene, sparing emoji) rather than generic engagement bait. Since X blocks scheduled auto-publish of tweets containing a raw link, the article URL is stripped from the body and posted as a separate link-reply tweet.
⊕
Threads — splits longer source material (especially EDM repurposing) into a real 2–4 post connected thread rather than one oversized post, with each segment hard-capped to Threads' 500-character limit.
in
LinkedIn — uses one of two voices: a thought-leadership prompt (varied hook styles — direct question, quoted claim, quoted customer voice — an optional mechanism analogy, and an optional closing brand-mantra line such as "Keep payments simple, just HitPay") for regular posts, or a distinct HitPay announcement voice (hook → bridge → mechanics → friction-removal → recap → CTA → optional mantra) for changelog and product-launch posts. Both voices ban markdown emphasis syntax (LinkedIn doesn't render it) and treat hashtags/emoji as optional rather than mandatory, matching HitPay's real published post patterns.
✉
From EDM — paste a newsletter and generate X + Threads drafts from it directly, without needing a blog post first.

Repurpose All generates X, Threads, and LinkedIn drafts from one post in a single action and schedules them all to the same next-weekday 9am SGT slot. Draft platforms already used for a post show as icons in the post list's "Repurposed For" column.

Quality guardrails — every generated post is checked for a proper closing sentence (no more dangling "the gap closed itself" with the full stop stripped off after a URL is removed), and merchant-story prompts (Threads stories, the X Wednesday merchant story slot) always inject a fresh, randomised business/location/name seed with an explicit instruction not to default to recurring examples — fixing an earlier pattern where stories kept reconverging on the same handful of characters (e.g. a guesthouse owner in El Nido, a textile shop in Chinatown, a merchant named "Maribel"). Repurpose All also spaces out scheduling by theme — if a similarly-themed post (by shared keyword overlap in the blog title) is already scheduled within 4 days, the new slot is pushed forward to the next available weekday so the social calendar doesn't cluster near-duplicate topics.

YouTube description generator

A standalone platform tab (▶ YouTube) for writing AEO-optimised YouTube video descriptions — unlike the repurposing tools above, it doesn't need an existing blog post as input.

1
Paste in whatever you have about the video — a summary, talking points, transcript notes, a customer quote — and optionally pick a market (SG MY PH or Global).
2
Claude writes the description and links "Learn more" to whichever published blog post is most relevant to the video and market, reusing the same internal blog-link matching used in post generation.
3
Every generated description is saved with its source video info, matched post, and editor, so past descriptions can be reopened, copied, or deleted from the History panel alongside the generator.
Editorial workflow
  • ✨ Generated — freshly generated, ready for review.
  • ✏️ Editing — in progress, being edited before approval.
  • 🔵 Ready to Publish — reviewed and approved for publishing.
  • ✅ Published — live on the blog.

The Lifecycle section in each post's sidebar records when it moved through each stage, and shows elapsed time between steps. The Analytics panel (header icon) charts posts generated and time spent per status over day/week/month periods, so the team can track content pipeline speed.

GA4 analytics — sessions, users, avg. engagement duration, and bounce rate per post, pulled live from Google Analytics 4 and keyed by slug (so market URL prefixes don't split the numbers), can be toggled on as extra columns in the Overview table or viewed inline on a single post's detail view. A shared time-range selector (7 / 30 / 90 days, or a custom range) controls both the table columns and the Analytics panel charts.

Use AI Edit to send a selected passage (or the full post) back to Claude with a specific instruction, without regenerating the whole article.

Export / Import CSV — export one or many posts to a Framer-CMS-format CSV. After editing fields externally, re-upload that CSV on a single post to sync the edited fields (title, slug, meta fields, categories, tags, body) straight back into it.
